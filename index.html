How a cup gets its score

A short primer on the numbers behind the form, drawn from the SCA (Specialty Coffee Association) cupping protocol this dataset follows.

Total cup points

Q-graders score ten sensory categories — fragrance/aroma, flavor, aftertaste, acidity, body, balance, uniformity, clean cup, sweetness and overall — each out of 10, then sum them. 100 is a perfect, and effectively theoretical, score.

Specialty threshold

80.00 is the line between "specialty" and commercial-grade coffee. Below it, the lot is graded on a separate commercial scale rather than a cupping sheet.

Defects

Category 1 defects (full black, full sour, pod, fungus damage) are graded out of the whole sample; a single one can be disqualifying. Category 2 defects (partial black, partial sour, broken, insect damage) are tolerated in small counts.

Quakers

Under-ripe cherries that don't roast properly — they stay pale and taste papery. Counted per 350g sample; more quakers usually track with a lower flavor and sweetness score.

Moisture

Green coffee is typically cupped between 9–13% moisture. Outside that band, beans risk tasting flat (too dry) or fermented and unstable in storage (too wet).

Altitude

Higher, slower-maturing cherries tend to develop denser sugars and more complex acidity — one reason altitude is a recurring, if imperfect, predictor of cup quality.
